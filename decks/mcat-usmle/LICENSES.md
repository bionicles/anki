# Credits and reuse

The combined medical study package includes work with different licenses. Distribute the compiled collection and OpenStax adaptations for noncommercial use under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, preserve these credits, and indicate changes. Each packaged note identifies its source and license. [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/)

**OpenStax, Rice University, and the contributing authors** provide the selected glossary definitions and textbook exercises. The adaptation selects chapters and terms, converts the markup to Anki fields, writes recall prompts for definitions, and maps questions to exam sections. The source revisions and section locators are in the note backs and source lock. The prefaces list the full writing and review teams.

**Histology images** are credited in the OpenStax figure captions to the Regents of the University of Michigan Medical School. The nine crops remove labels or isolate the tissue being tested. Each image card links to the exact textbook figure and carries the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 attribution. To reproduce a crop, use its JPEG from the pinned book’s media directory. Multiply the left and right fractions by the original width and the top and bottom fractions by the original height, round to the nearest pixel, crop, then thumbnail to fit 720 × 520. [Crop coordinates](specimens.tsv) · [Pinned source media](https://github.com/openstax/osbooks-anatomy-physiology/tree/f273762340768a5f50e833c876e40b5a9c565da1/media)

**CDC pathology photographs** come from the CDC’s Public Health Image Library and are available free from the CDC. Images 61, 71, 846, 861, 862, 863 and 865 are credited to Edwin P. Ewing, Jr., MD. Image 23482 is credited to Rosalie B. Haraszti, MD. Image 21261 is credited to CDC. The packaged PNGs resize the public-domain photographs to fit 750 × 570. Every corresponding card includes a direct link to its individual PHIL record and the provider credit. [CDC Public Health Image Library](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/phil/)

**Diagnostic imaging** comes from 92 individually credited images, including dermatology photographs, radiographs, CT, MRI, ultrasound, endoscopy, histology, blood and bone marrow films, and gross specimens on Wikimedia Commons. Each figure retains the license given by its contributor. The ledger identifies the original file, contributor, source URL, license, original file SHA-1, optional crop and output dimensions. To reproduce a PNG, verify the original checksum, apply the fractional crop if present and resize proportionally to fit 920 × 760. The image credit also appears on every card that uses it. [Image sources and licenses](imaging.tsv)

**Urology figures.** Fourteen original figures use simulated hormone, semen and corporal blood-gas reports, syphilis titers, tumor-marker series and ultrasound volumes. The renderer is `urology_studies()` in `figures.py`. The six clinical sonograms and two histology images have individual credits and source checksums. [Image sources and licenses](imaging.tsv)

**Behavioral-health figures.** Twelve original figures use simulated mood, psychosis and tic timelines, menstrual symptom diaries, arterial blood gases, phosphate measurements and respiratory observations. The renderer is `behavioral_courses()` in `figures.py`.

**Multisystem symptom figures.** Twenty-two original figures use fictional observations, monitor logs, diaries, laboratory results and growth records. The renderer is `multisystem_symptom_studies()` in `figures.py`. Clinical sources appear on the question backs.

**Clinical nutrition figures.** Twenty original teaching figures use simulated growth measurements, edema assessments, laboratory panels, supplement labels and feeding records. The renderer is `clinical_nutrition_studies()` in `figures.py`. Clinical sources appear on the card backs.

**Multisystem genetics and cancer figures.** Twenty original figures depict simulated biochemical assays, genetic and tumor stain reports, and postexercise motor responses. Question backs cite the sources for interpretation. The renderer is `multisystem_genetic_studies()` in `figures.py`.

**Multisystem physiology figures.** Twenty-one original teaching figures show simulated measurements for blood gas interpretation, fluid balance, electrolyte disorders and hemodynamic reassessment. The rendering function is `multisystem_physiology()` in `figures.py`. The clinical references are credited on the cards.

**Well-patient care figures.** Fourteen original charts and tables show simulated patient measurements, sleep diaries, activity summaries, functional interviews and food labels. The two head-growth charts plot the WHO boys’ reference values at selected ages from birth to 12 months alongside fictional patient records. The renderer is `preventive_care_studies()` in `figures.py`. [WHO simplified field table for boys](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/child-growth/child-growth-standards/indicators/head-circumference-for-age/sft_hcfa_boys_p_0_5.pdf)

**Dermatology figures.** Nine original figures use simulated temperature diaries, individual lesion durations, skin-examination areas and adult burn surveys. The renderer is `cutaneous_studies()` in `figures.py`. Eleven clinical photographs and micrographs have their own attributions. [Image sources and licenses](imaging.tsv)

**Musculoskeletal figures.** Nineteen original diagrams, plots and laboratory reports use simulated measurements. The renderer is in `musculoskeletal_reports()` and `musculoskeletal_studies()` in `figures.py`. Fifteen patient images are recorded separately in the diagnostic imaging ledger. [Image sources and licenses](imaging.tsv)

**Clinical immunology figures.** The eleven original images use synthetic measurements and idealized fluorescence distributions. The rendering code is in `immune_reports()` in `figures.py`.

**Medical evidence figures.** Two original charts display aggregate rates from published trials. Each card credits the study and the year. The other 28 evidence figures use simulated data. All 30 are generated by `figures.py`. [Diabetes Prevention Program](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa012512) · [SPRINT](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1511939)

**ECG recordings** are from George Moody and Roger Mark, MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, version 1.0.0, PhysioNet, 2005. The eight figures plot six-second excerpts of modified lead II. The card backs identify the record and time interval. To reproduce the strips, download the .hea and .dat files for records 100, 119, 202, 207, 228 and 231 from the dataset. From this deck directory run `uv run --no-project --with wfdb==4.3.0 --with matplotlib==3.10.8 python ecg.py /path/to/records`. The source database is available under the Open Data Commons Attribution License v1.0. Original hypothetical clinical prompts accompanying the recordings are by this project. [Dataset and attribution](https://physionet.org/content/mitdb/1.0.0/) · [Moody and Mark, IEEE Engineering in Medicine and Biology Magazine, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1109/51.932724) · [ODC-By 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/by/1-0/)

**Intrapartum recordings** are ten-minute windows from the CTU-UHB Intrapartum Cardiotocography Database v1.0.0, created by Václav Chudáček, Jiří Spilka, Miroslav Burša, Petr Janků, Lukáš Hruban, Michal Huptych and Lenka Lhotská. The seven plots use six records (1001, 1002, 1003, 1004, 1007 and 1008). The plots preserve the sampled time axis and leave missing fetal heart-rate values blank. Each card gives the record and minute interval. Download the .hea and .dat pairs, then run `uv run --no-project --with wfdb==4.3.0 --with matplotlib==3.10.8 python intrapartum.py --records /path/to/records`. The original teaching strips and clinical scenarios are labeled separately. [PhysioNet database](https://physionet.org/content/ctu-uhb-ctgdb/1.0.0/) · [Chudáček et al., BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2393-14-16) · [ODC-By 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/by/1-0/)

**Original material** in `originals.tsv`, the plotted stimuli and project code use the repository's MIT license. The original reading passages and cases were written for this collection. The official AAMC and USMLE sites supply exam frameworks and links to practice materials. [Repository license](../../LICENSE)

**Heart sounds.** Jorge Oliveira, Francesco Renna, Paulo Costa, Marcelo Nogueira, Ana Cristina Oliveira, Andoni Elola, Carlos Ferreira, Alipio Jorge, Ali Bahrami Rad, Matthew Reyna, Reza Sameni, Gari Clifford and Miguel Coimbra shared the CirCor DigiScope Phonocardiogram Dataset v1.0.3 through PhysioNet under ODC-By 1.0. Each excerpt presents eight consecutive cycles from one recording, resampled and level-normalized. Each companion plot shows the first four cycles with annotated S1 and S2. The recording labels support timing and shape judgments. The deck uses separate clinical cases for anatomical diagnoses. [Dataset](https://doi.org/10.13026/tshs-mw03) · [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1109/JBHI.2021.3137048) · [PhysioNet](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44360-026-00096-z) · [License](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/by/1-0/)

To regenerate the excerpts, download the WAV and TSV pairs named in the manifest into one directory and run `uv run --no-project --with scipy==1.15.3 --with matplotlib==3.10.8 python heart_sounds.py /path/to/records` here. The renderer verifies both source checksums and the annotated cycle boundaries. [Record IDs, checksums and windows](heart-sounds.tsv) · [Source files](https://physionet.org/files/circor-heart-sound/1.0.3/training_data/)
