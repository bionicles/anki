# Card design across the study decks

The seven shipped decks contain 32,794 cards. The original five-deck review checked each editable export for shared answers and bare yes/no keys, then inspected examples against their learning objectives. A repeated answer can be appropriate when two situations call for the same decision. The examples below record editorial judgments. The counts alone cannot measure learning or certify that every fact deserves review.

## Priorities for subsequent revisions

Series 65 has 128 remaining answers consisting solely of “Yes” or “No”, CMT has 32, Frontier ML has three, and ACSM-CPT and AWS have zero. Start with Series 65 cases where the explanation contains the decision the learner should retrieve. Keep a binary decision where acting or declining to act is itself the intended outcome. Check the source before changing a regulatory or safety condition. These counts cover exact, case-insensitive matches after stripping a terminal period.

For a novel example of a familiar skill, change something that can affect the answer. For interchangeable wording, keep one answer key and one schedule. When both directions matter, give each direction its own scheduled card. For a fact with no corresponding objective, practical decision or essential prerequisite, leave it out of the review queue. To evaluate a change, reserve a fresh case, return to it after a delay, and compare accuracy and review time. The [skill’s card patterns](../deck-builder/references/card-patterns.md) link the underlying studies and document the tradeoffs.
